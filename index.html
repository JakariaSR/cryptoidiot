Is crypto a good investment for beginners?

Maybe — for a small amount you can afford to lose completely. Crypto is volatile: prices can drop 50% in weeks and recover months later, or never. Most beginners do well treating it like a high-risk side bet (1–5% of savings), not a retirement plan. Our DCA calculator shows what steady, boring investing actually looks like.

How much should I invest as a beginner?

Only what you wouldn't miss if it went to zero. A common starting point is $10–50 on your first purchase, just to learn how wallets, exchanges and fees work — the education is worth more than the potential profit at that size. Never invest money needed for rent, debt, or emergencies.

Is Bitcoin safe?

Bitcoin the network has run since 2009 without being hacked — that part is remarkably safe. The risky part is everything around it: fake exchanges, phishing sites, lost passwords, and sending to the wrong address. Read our scam guide before buying anything and you'll avoid 95% of beginner losses.

What's the difference between Bitcoin and Ethereum?

Bitcoin is mainly digital gold — a store of value people hold. Ethereum is a platform other apps and tokens are built on, so it's more like digital infrastructure. Bitcoin is simpler and slower; Ethereum can do more and carries more complexity (and fees — see what gas fees are). Most beginners start with Bitcoin, then explore.

Do I have to pay taxes on crypto?

In most countries, yes — selling, swapping or spending crypto is usually a taxable event, even if you never withdrew to your bank. Rules vary a lot by country, so check your local regulations or a tax tool for your region. Keeping a simple record of every buy and sell from day one saves huge pain later.
