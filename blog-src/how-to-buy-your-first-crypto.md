You've read enough. You want to buy your first $20 of Bitcoin without getting scammed, overcharged, or lost in menus. This is the checklist.

## Step 1: Pick a reputable exchange

An exchange is where you swap regular money (USD, EUR, etc.) for crypto. For a first purchase, use a large, long-running exchange — think Coinbase, Kraken, or Binance, depending on what's available in your country.

- Check that it **legally operates in your country** and supports your local payment methods.
- Look up its history: has it ever been hacked, and if so, did it repay users?
- Ignore ads, DMs, and "agents" recommending some exchange you've never heard of. That's scam setup 101.

## Step 2: Verify your identity (KYC)

Legitimate exchanges require ID verification — passport or driver's license plus a selfie. It feels annoying; it's actually a good sign. Anonymous "no-KYC" platforms are where beginners lose money. Expect verification to take a few minutes to a couple of days.

## Step 3: Turn on security before depositing a cent

Do this *now*, not later:

1. **Unique password** — generated by a password manager, never reused.
2. **Two-factor authentication (2FA)** — use an authenticator app (Google Authenticator, Authy), not SMS. SIM-swap attacks target crypto holders specifically.
3. **Withdrawal address whitelist** — if the exchange offers it, lock withdrawals to your own wallet addresses only.
4. **Anti-phishing code** — set one so you can spot fake emails pretending to be the exchange.

## Step 4: Deposit a small amount

Start with an amount you can genuinely afford to lose — $20–50 is perfect for learning. Bank transfer usually has the lowest fees; cards and PayPal-style options are faster but cost more. Watch out for the fee screen before confirming.

## Step 5: Make your first purchase

Buy a well-known, highly liquid coin: **Bitcoin (BTC)** or **Ethereum (ETH)**. Beginners get tempted by cheap coins with wild promises. Every coin is divisible — you can own 0.001 BTC — so "price per coin" is irrelevant. A $10,000 coin and a $0.01 coin can grow or shrink by the same percentage.

- Use a **market order** (buys instantly at current price) for simplicity.
- Limit orders (set your own price) save a little on fees but can be confusing the first time.

## Step 6: Decide where it lives

Your coins now sit on the exchange (remember: *their* keys). For a tiny first purchase, that's acceptable while you learn. Once you hold a meaningful amount, withdraw to a wallet you control — a hot wallet app for convenience, a hardware wallet for serious savings. Test with a tiny withdrawal first, because sending to a wrong address is irreversible.

<div class="callout"><b>Golden rule of transfers:</b> crypto transactions cannot be undone. Always send a small test amount first, and double-check the first and last 5 characters of the address.</div>

## Step 7: Ignore everything else for 6 months

Not the influencer with a "guaranteed 10x." Not the Telegram group with insider signals. Not the NFT your cousin won't shut up about. Buy, secure it, and go live your life. Check in monthly. That boring approach has outlasted almost every exciting strategy in crypto history.

## The one-paragraph version

Choose a major exchange available in your country, complete ID verification, lock down your account with a password manager and app-based 2FA, deposit $20–50, buy BTC or ETH with a market order, and learn to self-custody before your balance gets big. Small first, always.
