You've probably seen it on every exchange: "Earn 5% APY on your ETH!" or "Stake now and get rewards!" It sounds like free money, which is exactly why you should slow down. **Staking is real — you genuinely can earn passive income from crypto you hold — but the yield is never free, and it always comes with risks.**

## What staking actually is

Some blockchains (the big ones: Ethereum, Solana, Cardano, and others) are secured by a system called **proof of stake**. Instead of miners burning electricity, coin holders "lock up" their coins to help run the network — validating transactions, producing new blocks. In return, the network pays them rewards.

That locking-up process is staking. Three things to know:

- **Your coins stay yours.** You're not lending them to a company (usually — more on that trap below). You're depositing them into the blockchain's own reward program.
- **Locked coins still move with the market.** Stake $1,000 of ETH, and if ETH's price drops 30%, your stake is now worth $700 *plus whatever rewards you earned*. Rewards don't protect you from price drops.
- **The reward rate is variable.** The advertised APY is a snapshot, not a promise. Rates drift with network activity and how many people are staking.

## Where does the yield come from?

This is the question that separates staking from scams. The yield comes from the blockchain itself: new coins issued to reward validators, plus a share of transaction fees. There's a real economic engine behind it.

That's very different from "guaranteed 20% yield" schemes, where your "interest" is paid by new depositors (a Ponzi) or by reckless gambling (see: the collapse of Celsius and similar lenders in 2022). A healthy rule of thumb:

- **2–6% APY on a major coin from the network itself** — normal staking, plausible.
- **"Guaranteed" double-digit or fixed returns** — walk away. Every time.

## How to actually stake (3 ways, easiest to hardest)

**1. Staking on an exchange (easiest).** Big exchanges offer one-click staking: you hold the coin, tap "stake," done. Downsides: they take a cut, you're trusting the exchange (remember: *not your keys, not your coins*), and some lock your funds for a period. Fine for small amounts and learning.

**2. Staking from your own wallet.** Wallets like the big self-custody ones let you stake directly to the network. Slightly more setup, but you keep control. On Ethereum there's one catch: solo staking requires 32 ETH (over $100,000) — so beginners use **pooled staking**, where many people combine funds to share a validator.

**3. Running a validator (advanced).** Your own hardware, 24/7 internet, real technical responsibility. Skip this until you know you need it.

<div class="callout"><b>The term to watch:</b> staking through an exchange is often <b>lending in disguise</b>. Ask one question — "who is running the validator?" If the answer is "the platform pools it," your risk isn't just the blockchain, it's the platform too. That's how platforms like Celsius failed and took customer funds with them.</div>

## The 4 risks nobody mentions in the ads

**1. Price risk is the big one.** A 5% APY on a coin that falls 40% is a 35% loss. Staking rewards are measured in *more coins*, not *more dollars*.

**2. Lock-up periods.** Many networks lock your coins for days to weeks when you unstake. You cannot sell during a crash if your coins are mid-unstaking. Check the unbonding period before you commit money you might need.

**3. Slashing.** If a validator misbehaves or goes offline, the network can destroy part of its stake. If you're staked through that validator, you share the loss. Quality pools make this rare, but it exists.

**4. The taxes.** In most countries, staking rewards are taxable income the moment you receive them, and a capital gain/loss when you sell. Ten small reward deposits a month can become a spreadsheet nightmare — a tax tool pays for itself fast here.

## Staking vs. just holding

| | Holding | Staking |
|---|---|---|
| Effort | Zero | Low (one setup, occasional checks) |
| Return | 0% | Roughly 2–7% APY on major coins |
| Extra risk | Market only | Market + lock-up + platform |
| Your coins available? | Anytime | Locked for an unstaking period |

For a beginner with a small, long-term position, staking a coin you'd hold anyway is a reasonable middle step — you're paid for patience you were already planning to have. For money you might need next month, the lock-up risk isn't worth a few percent.

## The one-paragraph version

Staking means locking up coins you own to help secure a proof-of-stake blockchain, and getting paid rewards by the network for it — think of it as interest for doing a job, not free money. You can do it on an exchange (easiest, but you're trusting them), from your own wallet (more control), or by running a validator (not for beginners). The catch: rewards of 2–6% won't save you if the coin's price drops, your coins get locked for days or weeks when you unstake, bad validators can lose a slice of your stake, and rewards are usually taxable. It's a sensible add-on for coins you're holding long-term anyway — not a reason to buy a coin you wouldn't otherwise hold.
