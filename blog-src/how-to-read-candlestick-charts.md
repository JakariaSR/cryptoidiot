Open any crypto chart and you'll see red and green candles marching across the screen like some kind of financial rune language. It looks intimidating. It isn't. **Every candlestick encodes just four numbers — the opening price, the closing price, the highest price, and the lowest price over a set period. That's the entire trick.**

## Anatomy of a single candle

Think of one candle as a summary of a fight between buyers and sellers during one time period:

- **The body** (the thick part) — the distance between the opening and closing price. Big body = one side won decisively. Small body = a stalemate.
- **The wicks** (the thin lines above and below) — how far the price traveled beyond the open/close before snapping back. Long wicks mean someone pushed hard and got rejected.
- **Green candle** — price closed *higher* than it opened. Buyers controlled the period.
- **Red candle** — price closed *lower* than it opened. Sellers controlled the period.

That's genuinely it. A candle can't tell you the future — it tells you who was winning during those 15 minutes, or those 4 hours, and how violently.

## Reading a market in 5 candles

Put candles in a row and they start telling a story. A few classic sequences:

**Strong uptrend:** consecutive green candles with growing bodies. Buyers keep arriving with force.

**Rejection:** a long upper wick after a climb — price shot up, sellers slammed it back down. Caution sign, not a verdict.

**Indecision:** a tiny-bodied candle (a "doji") with long wicks both ways. Buyers and sellers fought to a draw. The crowd is hesitating — bigger moves often follow.

**Exhaustion:** after a long run of huge green candles, bodies start shrinking. The buying party is running out of energy.

<div class="callout"><b>The most important skill:</b> learning what NOT to read. One candle is noise. Five candles are weather. Fifty candles are climate. Beginners stare at the 1-minute chart and see ghosts; zoom out to the daily or weekly chart and the same market suddenly looks calmer and more legible.</div>

## Timeframes: the same chart, different lies

Every chart lets you pick the candle period: 1 minute, 15 minutes, 1 hour, 1 day, 1 week. This matters more than any pattern:

- **Short timeframes (minutes–hours)** are dominated by noise, bots, and leverage gamblers. Wild swings mean little.
- **Daily candles** are the standard unit of actual market sentiment — one battle per day, one result.
- **Weekly/monthly** show the real trend and filter out almost all noise.

A useful habit: when you open a chart, always zoom out first. A coin "crashing" on the hourly chart may be a small blip on the weekly one — or the reverse. Context is everything.

## The pattern trap (read this before trading anything)

Search "candlestick patterns" and you'll find libraries of exotic names — hammer, shooting star, morning star, three black crows. Every one comes with a promised prophecy: *this shape predicts the next move.*

Here's the uncomfortable evidence: these patterns have modest predictive power at best, almost none in noisy markets, and zero ability to see news, regulation, hacks, or macro events — which are what actually move crypto prices. Trading on candle shapes alone is astrology with spreadsheets.

What charts *are* genuinely good for:

1. **Context** — is this coin in an [uptrend, downtrend, or chop](bull-vs-bear-markets.html)? Trend is real information.
2. **Volatility** — wide candles and long wicks tell you a coin is jumpy; position size accordingly.
3. **Levels** — prices where the market repeatedly bounces or stalls ("support" and "resistance") reveal where crowds made decisions before.

Use them for that. Leave the prophecy to the people selling courses.

## A beginner's 3-step chart routine

1. **Zoom out first.** Open the weekly, then daily chart. Ask: up, down, or sideways over the past months?
2. **Check the recent story.** Last 10–20 candles: is momentum building or fading? Any violent rejections?
3. **Note the key levels.** Mark where price has repeatedly turned. If you're buying, those zones are where the crowd has made decisions before — expect a fight there again. (Want a second opinion on the crowd's mood? Check our live [Fear & Greed Index](/tools/fear-greed-index.html).)

Then — and this is the part that actually makes money — decide your position size and exit plan *before* emotion gets involved. The chart informs; the plan protects.

## The one-paragraph version

A candlestick compresses four numbers — open, high, low, and close — into one shape: the body shows who won the period, the wicks show how far price traveled before being rejected, and green means buyers won while red means sellers did. Chains of candles reveal trends, momentum shifts, and indecision, but the timeframe you choose changes the story entirely, so always zoom out to the daily or weekly view before drawing any conclusion. Named patterns like hammers and dojis are far less predictive than their reputation, and no chart shape can see news or macro shocks — so use charts for context, volatility, and key price levels, never as a fortune-telling device. The traders who survive aren't the best pattern-readers; they're the ones who sized their positions so that being wrong was affordable.
