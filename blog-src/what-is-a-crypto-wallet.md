Here's the sentence that will save you hours of confusion: **a crypto wallet doesn't store your coins. It stores your keys.**

Your coins never actually live "in" anything. They live on the [blockchain](what-is-a-blockchain.html) — a giant public ledger that everyone can see. What the wallet holds is the **private key**: a secret code that proves those coins are yours and lets you move them.

## The key analogy that actually works

Think of the blockchain as a massive row of safety deposit boxes behind glass. Anyone can walk past and look inside any box (that's why blockchain explorers exist). But only the person holding the key can open a box and move what's inside.

- **Public key / address** = the box number. You can share it freely. People use it to send you crypto.
- **Private key** = the physical key. Anyone who has it owns your coins. Full stop.

## Hot wallets vs. cold wallets

**Hot wallets** (MetaMask, Trust Wallet, Phantom) run on an internet-connected device. They're free and convenient — perfect for small amounts and everyday use. But because they're online, they can theoretically be hacked, just like anything connected to the internet.

**Cold wallets** (Ledger, Trezor) are physical devices that keep your keys offline. To spend funds, you physically press buttons on the device. Malware on your computer can't reach the key because the key never touches your computer.

<div class="callout"><b>Beginner rule of thumb:</b> hot wallet for spending money (like cash in your pocket), cold wallet for savings (like a vault). Many people use both.</div>

## The seed phrase: 12–24 words that ARE your wallet

When you set up a wallet, it shows you a **recovery phrase** (also called a [seed phrase](what-is-a-seed-phrase.html)): 12 or 24 random words. This phrase is a human-readable version of your master private key — and our [cold storage guide](what-is-cold-storage.html) covers how to protect it long-term.

- Anyone with your seed phrase can restore your wallet on *any* device and take everything.
- No legitimate company, support agent, or giveaway will *ever* ask for it. Asking for your seed phrase is the single biggest red flag in crypto.
- Write it on paper (or stamp it in metal). Never store it in a photo, email, cloud note, or password manager tied to your email.

## Why exchanges aren't wallets

When you buy crypto on an exchange (Binance, Coinbase, Kraken), the coins sit in the exchange's wallet — with *their* keys. You have an IOU, not the keys. That's what people mean by **"not your keys, not your coins."**

Exchanges are convenient for buying and selling, and most are fine for small balances. But history is littered with exchanges that froze withdrawals or collapsed. For anything you'd genuinely miss, move it to a wallet you control.

## The one-paragraph version

A crypto wallet stores the secret keys that prove your coins on the blockchain are yours. Hot wallets are free apps for everyday amounts; cold wallets are offline devices for serious savings. Your 12–24 word seed phrase is the master key — guard it like cash, because to a scammer, it literally is.
