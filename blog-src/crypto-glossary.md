Crypto has a vocabulary problem: the industry loves jargon, and most explanations online assume you already know half of it. This glossary fixes that. **Forty-five terms, each defined in plain English, with links to full guides wherever we have one.** Skim it, bookmark it, and never nod along to a word you don't understand again.

## A

**Address** — The public string you share to receive crypto (like `bc1q...`). Safe to publish; it's the destination, not the key. See [private keys](what-is-a-private-key.html).

**Airdrop** — Free tokens sent to wallets to bootstrap a project or reward early users. Real ones only cost a gas fee to claim; ones demanding deposits or seed phrases are scams. → [What Is an Airdrop?](what-is-an-airdrop.html)

**Altcoin** — Any cryptocurrency that isn't Bitcoin — from Ethereum to the long tail of tiny tokens. → [What Is an Altcoin?](what-is-an-altcoin.html)

**APY (Annual Percentage Yield)** — The yearly reward rate for staking or lending, including compounding. A variable snapshot, never a promise. → [Staking APY Calculator](/tools/staking-apy-calculator.html)

**ATH (All-Time High)** — The highest price an asset has ever reached. Social media is loudest near ATHs, which is precisely why they're dangerous reference points.

## B

**Bear market** — A sustained period (usually months) of falling prices and pessimism, loosely defined as a 20%+ decline. → [Bull vs Bear Markets](bull-vs-bear-markets.html)

**Bitcoin** — The first cryptocurrency (2009): scarce digital money on a decentralized ledger, capped at 21 million coins. → [The Idiot's Guide to Bitcoin](idiots-guide-to-bitcoin.html)

**Block** — A batch of recent transactions added to the blockchain, roughly every few minutes.

**Blockchain** — A shared, unchangeable record book copied across thousands of computers. → [What Is a Blockchain?](what-is-a-blockchain.html)

**Bridge** — Software that moves coins between blockchains or layers. Useful, and a leading phishing trap — use only official bridges. → [Layer 2s](what-is-a-layer-2.html)

**Bull market** — A sustained period of rising prices and optimism, loosely 20%+ up from a recent low. → [Bull vs Bear Markets](bull-vs-bear-markets.html)

## C

**Coin** — A cryptocurrency native to its own blockchain (BTC, ETH, SOL). → [Coin vs Token](coin-vs-token.html)

**Cold wallet / cold storage** — Keys kept fully offline, usually a hardware device. The vault, as opposed to the spending-money hot wallet. → [Cold Storage](what-is-cold-storage.html)

**Consensus mechanism** — The rules by which a blockchain's thousands of computers agree on truth. The two big ones: proof of work and proof of stake. → [PoW vs PoS](what-is-proof-of-work.html)

**Crypto exchange** — A platform for buying, selling, and swapping crypto — either a company (CEX) or software (DEX). → [CEX vs DEX](cex-vs-dex.html)

## D

**DCA (Dollar-Cost Averaging)** — Investing a fixed amount on a fixed schedule regardless of price. The boring strategy that removes emotion from buying. → [What Is DCA?](what-is-dca.html) · [Calculator](/tools/dca-calculator.html)

**DeFi (Decentralized Finance)** — Lending, borrowing, and trading rebuilt as code on a blockchain, without banks. High innovation, high risk. → [What Is DeFi?](what-is-defi.html)

**DEX (Decentralized Exchange)** — An exchange that's software: trade from your own wallet, no account, no custodian — and no support if things go wrong. → [CEX vs DEX](cex-vs-dex.html)

**DYOR (Do Your Own Research)** — The community's standard disclaimer. Often sincere advice, occasionally an excuse to hype — the research part is what matters.

## E

**Ethereum** — The second-largest blockchain, designed as a platform for apps and smart contracts; its coin is ETH. → [Bitcoin vs Ethereum](bitcoin-vs-ethereum.html)

## F

**Fear & Greed Index** — A 0–100 daily reading of crypto market emotion. Extreme fear or greed is a signal to think, not to automatically trade. → [Live gauge](/tools/fear-greed-index.html)

**Fiat** — Government-issued money: dollars, euros, taka, yen. What you trade *for* crypto.

**FDV (Fully Diluted Valuation)** — Market cap if all coins that will ever exist were already circulating. Reveals future supply pressure. → [Market Cap vs Price](market-cap-vs-price.html)

**FOMO (Fear Of Missing Out)** — The emotion that makes people buy tops. Recognize it by the urgency; treat it as a warning, not a signal. → [Beginner Mistakes](common-crypto-mistakes.html)

## G

**Gas fee** — The fee paid to a blockchain's validators to process a transaction, famously variable on Ethereum. → [What Is Gas Fee?](what-is-gas-fee.html) · [ETH units](/tools/eth-unit-converter.html)

## H

**Halving** — The pre-programmed event (every ~4 years) that cuts Bitcoin's new supply in half. → [What Is the Halving?](what-is-bitcoin-halving.html) · [Countdown](/tools/bitcoin-halving-countdown.html)

**Hardware wallet** — A physical device that keeps private keys in a chip and never exposes them to the internet. → [Cold Storage](what-is-cold-storage.html)

**HODL** — A legendary typo turned philosophy: hold through volatility instead of panic-selling. Behaviorally sound, whatever the spelling.

**Hot wallet** — A wallet app on an internet-connected device. Convenient for small amounts; reachable by attackers 24/7. → [Wallets](what-is-a-crypto-wallet.html)

## K

**KYC (Know Your Customer)** — The identity verification (ID + selfie) that exchanges are legally required to perform. → [What Is KYC?](what-is-kyc.html)

## L

**Layer 2** — A network on top of a main blockchain that processes transactions cheaply and anchors to it for security (Arbitrum, Base). → [What Is a Layer 2?](what-is-a-layer-2.html)

**Liquidity** — How easily an asset trades without moving its price. High liquidity = easy in, easy out. Thin liquidity = prices that collapse when you sell.

## M

**Market cap** — Price × total supply: the real size of a project. The number to compare — never price alone. → [Market Cap vs Price](market-cap-vs-price.html)

**Meme coin** — A token whose value is internet culture and hype rather than any product. Lottery tickets wearing a joke's clothing. → [What Is a Meme Coin?](what-is-a-meme-coin.html)

**Mining** — Competing with computing power to add blocks to a proof-of-work chain (Bitcoin), earning rewards. → [Proof of Work](what-is-proof-of-work.html)

## N

**NFT (Non-Fungible Token)** — A blockchain certificate of ownership for a unique digital item — art, memberships, game items. → [What Is an NFT?](what-is-an-nft.html)

**Node** — A computer running blockchain software, holding a copy of the ledger. Thousands of independent nodes = no single point of control.

## O

**Oracle** — A service that feeds real-world data (like prices) to smart contracts. A critical dependency — and a known failure point in crashes.

## P

**Private key** — The secret code that proves ownership and signs transactions. Never share it; anyone holding it owns the funds. → [What Is a Private Key?](what-is-a-private-key.html)

**Proof of Stake (PoS)** — Consensus secured by locked coins (staking); cheating burns the stake. → [PoW vs PoS](what-is-proof-of-work.html)

**Proof of Work (PoW)** — Consensus secured by computing power (mining); rewriting history would require out-computing the world. → [PoW vs PoS](what-is-proof-of-work.html)

**Public key / address** — The shareable, verifiable counterpart to a private key. It receives; the private key sends.

## R

**Rug pull** — Developers hype a token, take investors' money, and vanish. The exit scam of crypto. → [What Is a Rug Pull?](what-is-a-rug-pull.html)

## S

**Satoshi** — The smallest unit of Bitcoin (0.00000001 BTC), named after its pseudonymous creator — also the creator's name itself. → [Sats Converter](/tools/sats-converter.html)

**Seed phrase** — The 12–24 words that back up an entire wallet. The master key; guard it with your life savings, because that's what it controls. → [What Is a Seed Phrase?](what-is-a-seed-phrase.html)

**Slippage** — The gap between the price you expected and the price you got on a trade, caused by price movement during execution. Worst on thinly traded tokens.

**Smart contract** — Code on a blockchain that executes automatically when conditions are met. DeFi, tokens, and NFTs are all built from them. → [What Is DeFi?](what-is-defi.html)

**Stablecoin** — A token designed to hold a steady value, usually $1 (USDT, USDC). → [What Is a Stablecoin?](what-is-a-stablecoin.html)

**Staking** — Locking coins to help secure a proof-of-stake network, earning rewards in return. → [What Is Staking?](what-is-staking.html)

## T

**Token** — An asset built on someone else's blockchain (ERC-20 tokens on Ethereum). Afternoon-of-coding easy to create — which explains both the innovation and the junk. → [Coin vs Token](coin-vs-token.html)

## V

**Volatility** — How violently prices swing. Crypto's signature feature: expect 20–30% moves as routine, not emergencies.

## W

**Wallet** — Software (or hardware) that stores your keys and lets you interact with blockchains. It holds keys, not coins. → [What Is a Crypto Wallet?](what-is-a-crypto-wallet.html)

**Whale** — An address holding enough crypto to move markets when it trades. Watching whales ≠ copying them profitably.

**Whitepaper** — A project's founding document: the problem, the design, the token. The first thing to read — and the first thing scammers fake.

## 5

**51% attack** — Controlling most of a network's mining power to rewrite recent transactions. A real threat to tiny chains; practically impossible against Bitcoin today. → [Can Bitcoin Be Hacked?](can-bitcoin-be-hacked.html)

## The one-paragraph version

You now hold the decoding ring: coins run their own blockchains while tokens ride on someone else's; wallets hold keys, not coins, with the seed phrase as master backup and the private key as the single secret that moves everything; exchanges are either companies holding your coins (CEX) or software letting you keep them (DEX); and the industry's whole economy — from stablecoins and staking to DeFi and NFTs — is built from smart contracts running on blockchains secured by proof of work or proof of stake. The jargon never stops coming, but it always reduces to a handful of ideas, and you now own all of them.
