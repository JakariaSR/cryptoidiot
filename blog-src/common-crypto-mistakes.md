Every crypto beginner makes mistakes. The expensive ones are almost never exotic — they're the same ten errors, repeated by millions of people, in every market cycle. The good news: reading a list like this is genuinely protective. **Most beginner losses aren't from bad luck; they're from predictable errors you can simply decide not to make.** Here are the ten, roughly in the order they get you.

## 1. Buying because the price looks "cheap"

"$0.0003 — imagine if it hits a penny!" This is the single most common and most destructive beginner mistake, and it's purely mathematical. Price per coin is meaningless without supply. A fraction of a penny can be wildly overvalued; $50,000 can be cheap. We've covered the full fix in [market cap vs price](market-cap-vs-price.html) — read it before buying anything.

## 2. Investing money you can't afford to lose

Rent money, emergency savings, borrowed funds — if losing it would damage your life, it doesn't belong in crypto. Crypto can drop 50% in a month *in a normal year*. Sizing your position so that a crash is annoying rather than catastrophic is the foundation every other rule stands on.

## 3. Keeping everything on an exchange

Exchanges are convenient and often fine — until they aren't. Frozen withdrawals, hacks, collapses: the [cold storage](what-is-cold-storage.html) ladder exists because history kept providing examples. Small active balances on an exchange: reasonable. Your entire net worth on one: a bet on that exchange's competence and honesty.

## 4. Falling for FOMO at the top

When the barber, the cousin, and the morning news are all talking about a coin, the easy money has usually been made. Buying vertical green candles because "it's going to the moon" is how beginners fund early sellers' retirements. The boring counter: [DCA](what-is-dca.html) — a fixed amount on a fixed schedule, decided calmly in advance.

## 5. Ignoring the seed phrase rules

Writing it nowhere, screenshotting it, typing it into a website "to verify." Each rule in the [seed phrase guide](what-is-a-seed-phrase.html) exists because breaking it has a body count. Two paper or metal copies, two separate locations, never digital, never shared. Ten minutes, once, protects everything.

## 6. Not doing the math on fees

Withdrawal fees, network fees, spread, swap slippage — crypto nickel-and-dimes you in more places than traditional finance. Small amounts are disproportionately eaten. Before moving anything, check what the total cost actually is; sometimes the "convenient" route costs 5% of a small transfer.

## 7. Selling in panic during crashes

Prices fall 30%, the headlines scream, and selling "before it's worse" feels like responsibility. Historically, it's been the most reliable way to convert temporary drops into permanent losses. Structure beats emotion: decide your strategy before the crash, not during it ([bulls and bears](bull-vs-bear-markets.html) always return).

## 8. Ignoring taxes until it's a problem

Selling, swapping, and spending crypto are [taxable events](crypto-taxes-for-beginners.html) in most countries. "I'll figure it out later" turns into a forensic reconstruction of two years of forgotten transactions — or worse, a letter from the tax authority. Log transactions as you go; April should be reporting, not archaeology.

## 9. Chasing airdrops, giveaways, and "free" crypto

If it's free and requires your wallet to connect, your phrase to "verify," or a deposit to unlock — it costs exactly what you give it. This is scam mechanics 101 from the [scam guide](common-crypto-scams.html). Legitimate airdrops exist, but they never ask for your seed phrase, and they never need you to send money first.

## 10. Telling everyone how much you have

Bragging on social media, discussing holdings in public groups, showing portfolio screenshots — all of it paints a target on you. Attackers comb exactly those places. The crypto-wealthy are quiet for the same reason the traditionally wealthy are: visibility invites risk. Talk process, never amounts.

<div class="callout"><b>The meta-lesson:</b> eight of these ten mistakes are <b>habits and structure</b>, not knowledge. You now have the knowledge — which puts you ahead of most people who lost money with better information than you had. What protects you is doing the boring parts consistently.</div>

## The checklist version (save this)

- [ ] Compare market caps, never raw prices
- [ ] Only invest what losing wouldn't change your life
- [ ] Move meaningful amounts to self-custody
- [ ] Buy on a schedule, not on hype
- [ ] Seed phrase: two copies, two locations, offline
- [ ] Check total fees before any transfer
- [ ] Pre-decide what you'll do in a crash
- [ ] Log transactions for tax season
- [ ] Never pay or share keys for "free" crypto
- [ ] Never publicize holdings

## The one-paragraph version

The ten mistakes that cost beginners the most are predictable: buying coins because the unit price looks cheap, investing money that matters, leaving everything on an exchange, FOMO-buying tops, breaking the seed phrase rules, ignoring stacked fees, panic-selling crashes, deferring taxes until they become a crisis, chasing free-crypto lures, and advertising your holdings. None require special intelligence to avoid — they require structure decided in calm moments: position sizes you can live with, a fixed buying schedule, two offline copies of your recovery words, records kept as you go, and a quiet mouth. The market will always offer ways to lose money; these ten are the ones you were going to be offered first.
