Everyone knows Bitcoin. The second name you'll hear constantly is Ethereum, and newcomers usually hit the same wall: *is it a coin? A company? A better Bitcoin?* The short answer: **Ethereum is a global computing platform that happens to have its own money — and that combination changed what crypto can do.** For the head-to-head angle, there's a full [Bitcoin vs Ethereum](bitcoin-vs-ethereum.html) breakdown; this one is just Ethereum, properly introduced.

## From digital gold to a world computer

Bitcoin's designer made one thing and made it extraordinarily well: money that no one controls. Ethereum, launched in 2015, asked a bigger question — what if the [blockchain](what-is-a-blockchain.html) could run **programs**, not just track coins?

The answer is the "smart contract": code stored on the blockchain that runs exactly as written, for anyone, with no company able to alter or cancel it. Money + programmable logic = applications that don't need a middleman:

- **Lending and borrowing** without banks ([DeFi](what-is-defi.html))
- **Exchanges** that run on code instead of companies ([CEX vs DEX](cex-vs-dex.html) covers the difference)
- **Digital items with real ownership** ([NFTs](what-is-an-nft.html))
- New tokens, launched by anyone, on shared infrastructure — which is why [altcoins](what-is-an-altcoin.html) mostly exist here rather than building their own networks

Bitcoin is primarily a monetary network. Ethereum is a general-purpose one — its pitch is less "digital gold" and more "the settlement layer for an internet of finance."

## Ether (ETH): the fuel, not just the money

Ethereum's native coin is Ether (ETH). It plays two roles people constantly blur:

1. **An asset people buy and hold**, like Bitcoin — which is why "Ethereum" and "ETH" get used interchangeably.
2. **Fuel for the machine.** Every action on Ethereum — sending tokens, using an app, minting something — costs [gas](what-is-gas-fee.html), priced and paid in ETH. The more the network is used, the more ETH gets consumed as fuel.

That second role is the key to "why does it have value" questions: ETH is the unavoidable resource every Ethereum transaction needs, the way you can't drive a car without fuel.

## The big upgrade most guides skip

In 2022, Ethereum changed how it secures the network from the mining model ([proof of work](what-is-proof-of-work.html)) to **proof of stake** — validators lock up ETH to secure the chain instead of burning electricity. The practical effects: roughly 99% less energy use, and ETH itself became something you can lock up to earn rewards, which is the foundation of [staking](what-is-staking.html).

You don't need to care about the mechanics. You need to know the network you'll actually use for most crypto apps is fast, comparatively cheap, and here to stay.

## What beginners should actually take from this

- Ethereum isn't "Bitcoin 2.0" — it's a different bet: general-purpose platform vs. purpose-built money.
- Most of the interesting (and dangerous) apps in crypto live on Ethereum or its [layer-2](what-is-a-layer-2.html) networks.
- ETH's dual role as asset-and-fuel makes it behave differently from Bitcoin — useful context before comparing prices, since [market cap vs price](market-cap-vs-price.html) explains why a "cheap-looking" coin isn't necessarily cheap.
- "Smart contract" is a famously misleading name: the code runs exactly as written, bugs included, which is why billions have been lost to flawed contracts — trust the code's audit, not its marketing.

## The one-paragraph version

Ethereum is the second-largest crypto network and the first major one designed as a general-purpose world computer: beyond tracking money, it runs "smart contracts" — unchangeable programs that let anyone build bankless lending, decentralized exchanges, NFTs, and new tokens on shared infrastructure. Its native coin, Ether (ETH), is both an investable asset and the mandatory fuel (gas) for every action on the network, which underpins its value. Since its 2022 proof-of-stake upgrade it's also energy-light and the base for staking. In short: Bitcoin is designed to be money; Ethereum is designed to be everything money can plug into — including, as it turns out, the traditional stock market itself.
