Every time you send crypto or use an app on a blockchain, you pay a fee. On Ethereum it's called **gas**. The name sounds technical, but the idea is simple: **you're paying the network's computers for their work.**

## Gas is just a processing fee

A blockchain isn't run by one company — it's run by thousands of computers around the world (called validators). Those computers spend real electricity and real hardware to process your transaction. Gas is how they get paid.

- **You pay gas in the network's native coin** — on Ethereum you pay in ETH, even if you're sending a different token.
- **The fee isn't fixed** — it changes minute by minute based on demand.
- **It goes to validators, not to a company** — there's no customer support to refund it.

## Why fees suddenly explode

Think of a blockchain like a highway with limited lanes. Every transaction is a car.

When few people are driving, fees are cheap — a few cents. But when everyone rushes in at once (a hot NFT drop, a market panic, a viral app), the highway jams. To get your transaction processed before everyone else's, you have to outbid them. Fees that cost 20 cents on a quiet Tuesday can hit $50 on a chaotic Saturday.

<div class="callout"><b>Key insight:</b> gas fees aren't high because blockchains are greedy — they're high because <b>block space is scarce</b>. When demand exceeds supply, prices spike. Simple economics, annoying consequences.</div>

## What actually determines the price?

On Ethereum, your total fee has two parts:

- **Base fee** — set automatically by the network. It goes up when the network is busy and down when it's quiet. This part gets *burned* (destroyed), not paid to anyone.
- **Priority fee (tip)** — an optional extra you add to jump the queue. Bigger tip, faster processing.

Other chains work similarly but cheaper. Solana fees are typically a fraction of a cent. Layer-2 networks (like Arbitrum or Base) sit on top of Ethereum and bundle thousands of transactions together, so their fees are usually under 10 cents.

## 4 ways to pay less gas

1. **Time your transactions.** Weekday afternoons (US time) and weekends are usually quieter. Sites like gas trackers show you the current price before you confirm.
2. **Use a Layer-2 network.** For swaps and transfers, moving to Arbitrum, Optimism, or Base can cut fees by 95%+.
3. **Batch when possible.** Some wallets let you combine multiple actions into one transaction — one fee instead of three.
4. **Set a max fee you're willing to pay.** Most wallets let you cap it. If the network gets busier than your cap, the transaction waits — better than overpaying in a panic.

## Gas on other blockchains

"Gas" is Ethereum's word, but every chain has fees with different personalities:

- **Bitcoin** — simpler: fee per transaction, based on data size, not complexity. Usually a dollar or less when quiet.
- **Solana** — famously cheap, often less than a cent.
- **BNB Chain** — usually under 10 cents.
- **Ethereum Layer-2s** — a few cents, inheriting Ethereum's security.

## The one-paragraph version

Gas is the fee you pay the network's computers to process your transaction. It's priced by supply and demand, so it spikes when everyone's using the network at once. You pay less by transacting at quiet times, using cheap networks like Solana or Ethereum Layer-2s, and setting a maximum fee in your wallet. It feels annoying at first — but once you see it as an auction for limited space, it stops being mysterious.
