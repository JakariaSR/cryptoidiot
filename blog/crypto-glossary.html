The Crypto Glossary: 45 Terms Explained in Plain English | Crypto Idiot

12 min read · Crypto Idiot Guides

Crypto has a vocabulary problem: the industry loves jargon, and most explanations online assume you already know half of it. This glossary fixes that. Forty-five terms, each defined in plain English, with links to full guides wherever we have one. Skim it, bookmark it, and never nod along to a word you don't understand again.

A

Address — The public string you share to receive crypto (like bc1q...). Safe to publish; it's the destination, not the key. See private keys.

Airdrop — Free tokens sent to wallets to bootstrap a project or reward early users. Real ones only cost a gas fee to claim; ones demanding deposits or seed phrases are scams. → What Is an Airdrop?

Altcoin — Any cryptocurrency that isn't Bitcoin — from Ethereum to the long tail of tiny tokens. → What Is an Altcoin?

APY (Annual Percentage Yield) — The yearly reward rate for staking or lending, including compounding. A variable snapshot, never a promise. → Staking APY Calculator

ATH (All-Time High) — The highest price an asset has ever reached. Social media is loudest near ATHs, which is precisely why they're dangerous reference points.

B

Bear market — A sustained period (usually months) of falling prices and pessimism, loosely defined as a 20%+ decline. → Bull vs Bear Markets

Bitcoin — The first cryptocurrency (2009): scarce digital money on a decentralized ledger, capped at 21 million coins. → The Idiot's Guide to Bitcoin

Block — A batch of recent transactions added to the blockchain, roughly every few minutes.

Blockchain — A shared, unchangeable record book copied across thousands of computers. → What Is a Blockchain?

Bridge — Software that moves coins between blockchains or layers. Useful, and a leading phishing trap — use only official bridges. → Layer 2s

Bull market — A sustained period of rising prices and optimism, loosely 20%+ up from a recent low. → Bull vs Bear Markets

C

Coin — A cryptocurrency native to its own blockchain (BTC, ETH, SOL). → Coin vs Token

Cold wallet / cold storage — Keys kept fully offline, usually a hardware device. The vault, as opposed to the spending-money hot wallet. → Cold Storage

Consensus mechanism — The rules by which a blockchain's thousands of computers agree on truth. The two big ones: proof of work and proof of stake. → PoW vs PoS

Crypto exchange — A platform for buying, selling, and swapping crypto — either a company (CEX) or software (DEX). → CEX vs DEX

D

DCA (Dollar-Cost Averaging) — Investing a fixed amount on a fixed schedule regardless of price. The boring strategy that removes emotion from buying. → What Is DCA? · Calculator

DeFi (Decentralized Finance) — Lending, borrowing, and trading rebuilt as code on a blockchain, without banks. High innovation, high risk. → What Is DeFi?

DEX (Decentralized Exchange) — An exchange that's software: trade from your own wallet, no account, no custodian — and no support if things go wrong. → CEX vs DEX

DYOR (Do Your Own Research) — The community's standard disclaimer. Often sincere advice, occasionally an excuse to hype — the research part is what matters.

E

Ethereum — The second-largest blockchain, designed as a platform for apps and smart contracts; its coin is ETH. → Bitcoin vs Ethereum

F

Fear & Greed Index — A 0–100 daily reading of crypto market emotion. Extreme fear or greed is a signal to think, not to automatically trade. → Live gauge

Fiat — Government-issued money: dollars, euros, taka, yen. What you trade for crypto.

FDV (Fully Diluted Valuation) — Market cap if all coins that will ever exist were already circulating. Reveals future supply pressure. → Market Cap vs Price

FOMO (Fear Of Missing Out) — The emotion that makes people buy tops. Recognize it by the urgency; treat it as a warning, not a signal. → Beginner Mistakes

G

Gas fee — The fee paid to a blockchain's validators to process a transaction, famously variable on Ethereum. → What Is Gas Fee? · ETH units

H

Halving — The pre-programmed event (every ~4 years) that cuts Bitcoin's new supply in half. → What Is the Halving? · Countdown

Hardware wallet — A physical device that keeps private keys in a chip and never exposes them to the internet. → Cold Storage

HODL — A legendary typo turned philosophy: hold through volatility instead of panic-selling. Behaviorally sound, whatever the spelling.

Hot wallet — A wallet app on an internet-connected device. Convenient for small amounts; reachable by attackers 24/7. → Wallets

K

KYC (Know Your Customer) — The identity verification (ID + selfie) that exchanges are legally required to perform. → What Is KYC?

L

Layer 2 — A network on top of a main blockchain that processes transactions cheaply and anchors to it for security (Arbitrum, Base). → What Is a Layer 2?

Liquidity — How easily an asset trades without moving its price. High liquidity = easy in, easy out. Thin liquidity = prices that collapse when you sell.

M

Market cap — Price × total supply: the real size of a project. The number to compare — never price alone. → Market Cap vs Price

Meme coin — A token whose value is internet culture and hype rather than any product. Lottery tickets wearing a joke's clothing. → What Is a Meme Coin?

Mining — Competing with computing power to add blocks to a proof-of-work chain (Bitcoin), earning rewards. → Proof of Work

N

NFT (Non-Fungible Token) — A blockchain certificate of ownership for a unique digital item — art, memberships, game items. → What Is an NFT?

Node — A computer running blockchain software, holding a copy of the ledger. Thousands of independent nodes = no single point of control.

O

Oracle — A service that feeds real-world data (like prices) to smart contracts. A critical dependency — and a known failure point in crashes.

P

Private key — The secret code that proves ownership and signs transactions. Never share it; anyone holding it owns the funds. → What Is a Private Key?

Proof of Stake (PoS) — Consensus secured by locked coins (staking); cheating burns the stake. → PoW vs PoS

Proof of Work (PoW) — Consensus secured by computing power (mining); rewriting history would require out-computing the world. → PoW vs PoS

Public key / address — The shareable, verifiable counterpart to a private key. It receives; the private key sends.

R

Rug pull — Developers hype a token, take investors' money, and vanish. The exit scam of crypto. → What Is a Rug Pull?

S

Satoshi — The smallest unit of Bitcoin (0.00000001 BTC), named after its pseudonymous creator — also the creator's name itself. → Sats Converter

Seed phrase — The 12–24 words that back up an entire wallet. The master key; guard it with your life savings, because that's what it controls. → What Is a Seed Phrase?

Slippage — The gap between the price you expected and the price you got on a trade, caused by price movement during execution. Worst on thinly traded tokens.

Smart contract — Code on a blockchain that executes automatically when conditions are met. DeFi, tokens, and NFTs are all built from them. → What Is DeFi?

Stablecoin — A token designed to hold a steady value, usually $1 (USDT, USDC). → What Is a Stablecoin?

Staking — Locking coins to help secure a proof-of-stake network, earning rewards in return. → What Is Staking?

T

Token — An asset built on someone else's blockchain (ERC-20 tokens on Ethereum). Afternoon-of-coding easy to create — which explains both the innovation and the junk. → Coin vs Token

V

Volatility — How violently prices swing. Crypto's signature feature: expect 20–30% moves as routine, not emergencies.

W

Wallet — Software (or hardware) that stores your keys and lets you interact with blockchains. It holds keys, not coins. → What Is a Crypto Wallet?

Whale — An address holding enough crypto to move markets when it trades. Watching whales ≠ copying them profitably.

Whitepaper — A project's founding document: the problem, the design, the token. The first thing to read — and the first thing scammers fake.

5

51% attack — Controlling most of a network's mining power to rewrite recent transactions. A real threat to tiny chains; practically impossible against Bitcoin today. → Can Bitcoin Be Hacked?

The one-paragraph version

You now hold the decoding ring: coins run their own blockchains while tokens ride on someone else's; wallets hold keys, not coins, with the seed phrase as master backup and the private key as the single secret that moves everything; exchanges are either companies holding your coins (CEX) or software letting you keep them (DEX); and the industry's whole economy — from stablecoins and staking to DeFi and NFTs — is built from smart contracts running on blockchains secured by proof of work or proof of stake. The jargon never stops coming, but it always reduces to a handful of ideas, and you now own all of them.
