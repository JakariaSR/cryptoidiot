What Is DCA? Dollar-Cost Averaging for Beginners | Crypto Idiot

5 min read · Crypto Idiot Guides

DCA stands for dollar-cost averaging, and it's gloriously unsexy: you invest a fixed amount of money on a fixed schedule, no matter what the price is doing. $50 of Bitcoin every Monday. That's it. That's the strategy.

Why "just buy when it's low" doesn't work

Everyone plans to buy low and sell high. The problem: you only know a low was the low after it's gone. In the moment, a dip looks identical to a crash, and a rally looks identical to a bubble. Timing the market requires being right twice — when you buy and when you sell — and even professionals are bad at it.

DCA removes the guessing. When the price is high, your $50 buys less. When the price is low, your $50 buys more. Over time, your average cost drifts toward the average price — which, for assets that trend upward over long periods, is exactly where you want to be.

A tiny example

Imagine $100 buys each month into a coin with a wild price:

  • Month 1: price $100 → you get 1.0 coin
  • Month 2: price $50 → you get 2.0 coins
  • Month 3: price $200 → you get 0.5 coins

You spent $300 and own 3.5 coins — an average cost of $85.71 per coin, even though the price "averaged" $116.67 across those months. The cheap purchases pulled your average down more than the expensive ones pushed it up. That asymmetry is the whole trick.

What DCA actually fixes: you

The real enemy isn't the market — it's your own emotions. FOMO makes people buy tops ("it's going to the moon, get in now!"). Fear makes people sell bottoms ("it's over, cut my losses"). Both feel rational in the moment and are reliably expensive.

A fixed schedule is a pre-commitment device. The decision was made once, calmly, in advance. When the chart turns red and scary, you don't make a new decision — you just follow the old one. Boring. Effective.

How to actually set it up

  1. Pick an amount you won't miss. $20 a week is a fine start. Crypto should be money you're genuinely okay losing entirely.
  2. Pick a schedule and automate it. Most exchanges let you set recurring buys. Automation beats willpower.
  3. Pick boring assets. DCA shines on established coins (BTC, ETH), not on a coin someone shilled in a group chat.
  4. Set a review cadence — then leave it alone. Monthly or quarterly. Daily checking is how DCA becomes day trading with extra steps.

The honest downsides

  • If the price only goes straight up, lump-sum investing beats DCA (you bought everything cheaper earlier). But nobody knows when "only up" is happening — that's the point.
  • DCA into a fundamentally bad asset just means losing money on a schedule. It's a buying method, not a magic spell.
  • Recurring buys can come with small fees per purchase — check your exchange's fee structure.

The one-paragraph version

Dollar-cost averaging means investing a fixed amount on a fixed schedule regardless of price. It buys more when things are cheap and less when they're expensive, and — more importantly — it takes your emotions out of the driver's seat. Automate it, check it monthly, and let time do the heavy lifting.
