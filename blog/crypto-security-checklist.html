The Crypto Security Checklist: 15 Habits That Keep Your Coins Yours | Crypto Idiot

7 min read · Crypto Idiot Guides

Crypto security advice is scattered across a dozen guides, so here's everything that actually matters in one place. This is the checklist version of everything our mistakes guide and scam guide teach — the habits, in order, that keep coins from becoming someone else's. Work down it once; revisit it quarterly. Ten minutes of maintenance beats years of regret.

Tier 1: Non-negotiables (do these today)

1. Seed phrase: two copies, two locations, offline. Paper or metal — never photos, cloud notes, email, or password managers. One copy at home (hidden, fire-conscious), one elsewhere (trusted family, safe deposit box). Full reasoning in the seed phrase guide.

2. App-based 2FA everywhere, never SMS. Authenticator apps (Aegis, Authy, Google Authenticator) — not text messages. SIM-swap attacks target crypto holders specifically, and phone-number recovery is the weakest link in most accounts.

3. Unique passwords from a password manager. Every exchange, every email, every service — different, generated, stored in a manager. One reused password turns any unrelated site breach into a crypto account breach.

4. Anti-phishing codes and withdrawal whitelists. Set the anti-phishing code on every exchange (so fake emails are instantly obvious) and whitelist withdrawal addresses (so a hijacked account can't send anywhere new without delay).

5. Bookmark the real sites; never click search ads for wallets or exchanges. Sponsored results and lookalike domains are the classic theft vector. Type or bookmark — never click through.

Tier 2: Strong habits (this week)

6. Separate your money: spending vs savings. A hot wallet app for everyday amounts, cold storage for anything you'd genuinely miss. Never all in one place, never all on an exchange.

7. Test every significant transfer. First move to any new address: small amount, confirm arrival, then the rest. Irreversibility makes verification cheap insurance.

8. Verify addresses on device screens, not just the app. Malware swaps clipboard addresses; hardware wallets show the real destination. Confirm the first and last characters match what you intended.

9. Update wallet apps and firmware — from official sources only. Updates patch real vulnerabilities. Fake "update" prompts are also a scam vector: only update from inside the app or the official site you bookmarked.

10. Review connected apps and approvals. Wallets accumulate permissions to old DeFi sites and airdrop claims. Periodically revoke permissions you no longer use (your wallet's interface has a "connected sites" or token-approval section).

Tier 3: Ongoing awareness (every quarter)

11. Be boring in public. No portfolio screenshots, no holdings talk, no "look what I bought" posts. Visibility invites targeting. Discuss ideas, never amounts.

12. Treat every unsolicited message as hostile. DMs "from support," "from the team," "from a helpful expert" — all of them, always. Real support never DMs first; the scam playbook depends on you forgetting this.

13. Verify unusual requests through a second channel. "Urgent" transfer, "verify your wallet," a friend suddenly asking about crypto out of character? Confirm via a different channel — call them, message the official account. Urgency plus secrecy is the scam signature.

14. Keep a simple asset map (offline). What you hold, roughly where, and what the recovery path is — stored like your seed phrase, offline. If something happens to you, this is also what helps your family (more on this below).

15. Update your emergency plan. Crypto's dark edge: if you disappear, your coins may disappear with you. A sealed letter with a family member or a proper inheritance setup (many wallets support timelocks or multi-signature recovery) turns your security from a trap into a legacy.

The 80/20 of crypto security: items 1–5 prevent the overwhelming majority of real-world thefts. Everything after that is refinement. If you do nothing else this week, do the first five — they're under an hour combined.

The quarterly 10-minute audit

Set a recurring reminder. Each quarter:

  • Seed phrase copies still where they should be, legible, intact
  • 2FA active on every exchange; recovery codes stored offline
  • Withdrawal whitelists still enabled; review the listed addresses
  • Wallet apps and device firmware current
  • Old token approvals revoked; connected sites reviewed
  • Asset map still accurate; emergency letter accessible to the right person

The one-paragraph version

Crypto security reduces to a short list of habits done consistently: protect your seed phrase with two offline copies in two locations, use app-based 2FA and unique generated passwords everywhere, set anti-phishing codes and withdrawal whitelists on exchanges, bookmark real sites instead of clicking ads, separate spending money from savings across hot and cold wallets, test transfers and verify addresses before sending, keep software updated from official sources, revoke stale wallet permissions, stay quiet about holdings, and treat every unsolicited message as hostile until proven otherwise through a second channel. Do the first five today — they block the vast majority of real thefts. Then run the ten-minute quarterly audit so security stays a system instead of a memory.
