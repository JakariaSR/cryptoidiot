What Is an Altcoin? Every Crypto That Isn't Bitcoin, Explained | Crypto Idiot

6 min read · Crypto Idiot Guides

Bitcoin was first, and everything after it needed a name. The crypto world chose a blunt one: altcoin — short for "alternative coin," meaning any cryptocurrency that isn't Bitcoin. Ethereum is an altcoin. So are Solana, Cardano, Dogecoin, and the other ten thousand tokens listed on tracking sites. One word covers a category so diverse it includes world-changing infrastructure, honest experiments, pointless clones, and outright scams — so the word alone tells you almost nothing. The categories inside it matter.

Why Bitcoin got its own category

The split isn't arbitrary. Bitcoin is genuinely different in kind from everything else:

  • It has no founder in control — Satoshi disappeared; no company runs it.
  • Its purpose never changed — digital money, nothing more.
  • Its supply and rules are the most conservative in crypto — changes require overwhelming consensus.

Every other project, to some degree, has a team, a company, a founder, a pivot, or a promise. "Altcoin" is shorthand for "crypto with people behind it" — which cuts both ways: faster innovation, but also central points of failure (see the exchange collapses and rug pulls that define altcoin history).

The main categories of altcoins

The chaos becomes legible when you sort altcoins by what they actually do:

Major platforms (ETH, SOL, and peers). Blockchains competing to be the base layer for applications — the "operating systems" of crypto. Their coins (tokens, technically, on their own chains they're coins) pay for network use. This is the second-most-established category after Bitcoin itself.

Stablecoins (USDT, USDC). Designed to hold a fixed value — covered fully in our stablecoin guide. Technically altcoins, functionally a different asset class.

Utility tokens. Access passes to a service: pay fees, unlock features, vote in a protocol. Value depends entirely on the service getting used.

Governance tokens. Voting shares in a DeFi protocol or DAO — the crypto version of a shareholder ballot.

Meme coins (DOGE, PEPE…). Jokes and culture as assets — our meme coin guide covers the mechanics and the danger.

Exchange tokens (BNB and peers). Issued by exchanges, used for fee discounts and platform perks. Their value leans heavily on one company's fortunes.

The long tail. Thousands of small projects, experiments, clones, and scams — this is where the rug pull factories live.

The brutal base rate: why most altcoins fail

Here's the statistic every altcoin investor should memorize: historically, the overwhelming majority of altcoins decline toward zero. In each market cycle, a handful of top projects survive and thrive, a batch stays mediocre, and the vast majority — often 90%+ of everything listed — fades to irrelevance.

The mechanisms are consistent:

  • No durable demand. A token needs a reason to exist beyond speculation. Most never find one.
  • Team abandonment. When the hype money runs out, unpaid founders move on.
  • Supply games. Insider unlocks and inflation schedules quietly dilute retail holders.
  • Better competition. Crypto reinvents itself fast; today's platform is tomorrow's relic.

None of this means altcoins are pointless — Ethereum, Solana, and others have delivered genuine innovation and real returns for early believers. It means the default outcome for any given altcoin is failure, and the burden of proof is always on the project, never on your skepticism.

How should a beginner think about altcoins?

A practical framework that most experienced people would endorse:

  1. Bitcoin first, altcoins later. Master buying, storing, and surviving volatility with the most established asset before adding complexity.
  2. Size them as the risky bucket. Within a crypto allocation, most beginners do best treating Bitcoin as the core and altcoins as a small satellite — the part that can go to zero without wrecking the plan.
  3. Judge by the same standards as Bitcoin skeptics use on Bitcoin: Is there a real use? Who controls it? What's the supply schedule? What happens if the team quits? The 5-minute project check filters most of the tail.
  4. Never chase the new listing. By the time an obscure altcoin reaches your feed, the insiders are usually looking for exit liquidity — see pump-and-dumps.
The one-line mental model: Bitcoin is a bet on one idea; altcoins are bets on ten thousand projects. A portfolio can hold both — but only if it sizes them for what they are: established thesis versus speculative venture.

The one-paragraph version

An altcoin is any cryptocurrency that isn't Bitcoin — a category that ranges from foundational platforms like Ethereum and Solana through stablecoins, utility and governance tokens, meme coins, and a long tail where most projects historically go to die. The category exists because Bitcoin is genuinely different: no founder in control, an unchanging mission, and the most conservative rules in crypto, while every altcoin has people, promises, and pivots behind it. The base rate is brutal — in every cycle the vast majority of altcoins decline toward zero — so beginners do best buying Bitcoin first, sizing altcoins as a small speculative satellite, judging projects with the same hard questions any investor should ask, and never chasing listings the crowd has already discovered. Bitcoin is a bet on an idea; each altcoin is a bet on a team. Portfolios can hold both, priced accordingly.
