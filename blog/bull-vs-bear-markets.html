Bull vs Bear Market: What the Animal Metaphors Actually Mean | Crypto Idiot

6 min read · Crypto Idiot Guides

Open any crypto chart and within minutes someone will mention bulls and bears. The words sound like insider code, but the idea is ancient market slang. A bull market is a long period of rising prices and optimism. A bear market is a long period of falling prices and pessimism. Everything else — the strategies, the psychology, the mistakes — flows from those two definitions.

Why a bull and a bear, of all animals?

Two stories compete, and both help you remember:

  • The bull attacks by thrusting its horns upward. Bull market = prices pushed up.
  • The bear attacks by swiping its paws downward. Bear market = prices swiped down.

Finance has used the pair for centuries — long before crypto existed. Once you picture the horns and the claws, you'll never mix them up again.

The official-ish definitions

There's no crypto police enforcing these, but the standard thresholds:

  • Bull market: prices up 20%+ from a recent low, with sustained optimism. In crypto, bull phases have historically run in long cycles, often peaking roughly every four years around Bitcoin's "halvings."
  • Bear market: prices down 20%+ from a recent high, sustained for weeks or months. Crypto bear markets are famously brutal — drops of 70–85% from the peak have happened more than once and are considered "normal" for this asset class.
  • Correction: a shorter, shallower dip (usually 10–20%) inside a larger trend. Every bull market contains dozens of scary corrections; surviving them without panic-selling is half the skill.
The beginner's trap: a 20% drop inside a bull market feels identical to the start of a bear market while it's happening. Nobody rings a bell at the top or the bottom. Anyone who claims to know exactly which one this is — is guessing, selling something, or both.

What actually drives each animal

Markets run on a feedback loop between price and mood:

Bull engine: prices rise → headlines get exciting → new people buy → demand pushes prices higher → early holders take profits and brag → more new people buy. News stories turn from "is Bitcoin dead?" to "your barber has a coin." That last one — when everyone, including people who never cared, is suddenly an expert — is historically a late-bull-market symptom.

Bear engine: prices fall → fear spreads → people sell to "get out before it's worse" → selling pushes prices lower → headlines get apocalyptic → weak hands exit at the bottom. The same herd instinct that pumped the bubble now tramples it.

Neither animal is rational on any given day. Both are rational over long periods. That's why timing day-to-day moves is a casino, while boring multi-year habits (like DCA) survive both animals.

How beginners should behave in each

During a bull market:

  1. Resist FOMO. Rising prices change how gains feel — $1,000 profit feels like proof you're a genius. It isn't; the tide is lifting everything. Genius is what people feel right before over-betting.
  2. Take profits on the way up. Decide in advance: "if my holdings double, I sell 20%." Written rules beat euphoria.
  3. Expect the 30% dips. They will come, they'll feel like the end, and in bull markets they've historically been buying opportunities for steady buyers.

During a bear market:

  1. Don't sell in panic assets you'd otherwise hold for years — that's how people crystallize temporary drops into permanent losses.
  2. Only keep buying what you'd hold anyway. "Cheap" can always get cheaper in crypto; a falling price is not, by itself, a reason to buy.
  3. Audit your security, not your portfolio. Bear markets are famously when exchanges fail and scams spike — the predators know the crowd is emotional.

In both: never invest money you need within a few years, and ignore anyone promising to time the turn for you.

Why it matters for your strategy

Here's the honest math of crypto cycles: prices that fall 80% need to rise 400% just to break even. That asymmetry is why bear-market panic-selling is so destructive — and why position sizing (only risking what you can stomach losing) matters more than any prediction.

If you accept that you will live through both animals — crypto guarantees it — then the winning strategy isn't prediction. It's structure: fixed-amount, fixed-schedule buying through the bear, pre-written profit rules through the bull, and an iron rule against borrowing or betting essentials in either.

The one-paragraph version

A bull market is a sustained stretch of rising prices and optimism; a bear market is a sustained stretch of falling prices and fear, with 20% being the rough dividing line and corrections as the noisy dips in between. Bull runs attract crowds, latecomers, and eventual euphoria; bear runs trigger panic-selling, exchange failures, and despair — both driven by the same herd instinct running in opposite directions. Nobody reliably knows when either animal arrives or leaves, so the beginner's edge isn't prediction: it's structure. Buy steadily through the bear with money you don't need, follow pre-written profit rules through the bull, and never let either animal talk you into betting what you can't afford to lose.
