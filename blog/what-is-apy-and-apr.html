APY vs APR: The Difference That Quietly Costs You Money | Crypto Idiot

5 min read · Crypto Idiot Guides

Every crypto app that offers rewards — staking, lending, savings-style products — advertises a percentage. And those percentages come in two flavors, APR and APY, that sound identical but aren't. The difference is compounding, and at crypto-scale interest rates it can quietly cost (or earn) you a lot more than you think. Five minutes here saves real money.

APR: the simple number

APR (Annual Percentage Rate) is the plain yearly rate, without compounding. Earn 10% APR on $1,000 → $100 over a year. Simple, linear, no tricks in the math itself.

APY: the compounding number

APY (Annual Percentage Yield) includes compounding — your earnings earning their own earnings, over and over.

Same 10%, but compounded monthly: ~10.47% effective. Compounded daily: ~10.52%. At normal bank-like rates the gap is small. But crypto yields run high, and the gap grows with the rate:

Advertised If it's APR If it's APY
5% $50 on $1,000 $50 on $1,000
10% $100 $105
20% $200 $221
50% $500 $648

That's why everyone advertises APY — it's the bigger-looking number for the same underlying rate. It's not a lie; it's a spotlight. When comparing two offers, convert them to the same measure first, or you're comparing apples to slightly larger apples.

The rule of thumb conversion

Roughly: APY ≈ APR + (APR² ÷ 2) for frequent compounding. So 20% APR ≈ 22% APY; 50% APR ≈ 62.5% APY. Or just use any APR-to-APY calculator and stop doing mental math with money.

Now, the part that actually matters more

Here's the honest truth about all crypto yield numbers, whatever the label: a percentage is a description, not a promise. The rate tells you what you'd earn if conditions stayed identical for a year — in a market where conditions change by the hour. Rates on crypto yield products drop, spike, and vanish constantly. The app quoting 20% today is quoting something else next month.

And beyond that, the entire history of crypto yield can be summarized as: the higher the APY, the more risk someone is taking with your money — often without telling you. Double-digit "guaranteed" yields are how Celsius, BlockFi-style products, and a hundred smaller ones ended with customers losing everything. "Where does the yield come from?" is the single best question in crypto, and "because of magic internet money" is never the answer. If nobody can explain the source of the yield in plain sentences, the yield is the bait.

This connects directly to the oldest pattern in the space — common crypto scams and rug pulls almost always advertise a shiny APY. Real yield comes from real activity (fees, network rewards, lending demand), and real yield is usually boring.

How to compare offers without getting played

  1. Same measure. Convert everything to APY before comparing.
  2. Source of yield. Fees? Staking rewards? Lending? "Trade volume" you can't verify? One sentence, plain English, or walk away.
  3. Variable vs fixed. Crypto rates are almost never locked; a "20% APY" banner next to an asterisk that says "rate subject to change" means exactly that.
  4. The token itself. If rewards are paid in some obscure token, you're being paid in the thing they want you to hold — price that token's volatility into the math.
  5. Custody. If the product holds your funds, everything in hot wallet vs cold wallet applies, plus counterparty risk on top.

The one-paragraph version

APR is a simple yearly rate without compounding; APY includes compounding, so the same underlying rate always looks higher as APY — convert everything to one measure before comparing. More importantly, crypto yield percentages are snapshots, not promises, and rate and risk travel together: boring single-digit yields from visible sources (fees, staking) are normal, while double-digit "guaranteed" APYs are historically how platforms lure deposits before the model breaks. Judge any offer by the plain-English source of its yield, not the size of its number — the percentage is the wrapping, the risk is the gift.
